Week 1: Set the Bar High

Before AI starts doing work for you, learn how to ensure the work is up to your standards. This week focuses on prompting fundamentals using real VA UX scenarios.

Day 0 Set up your AI workspace ▼
Before we start training, let's get your tools ready. You'll need access to an AI assistant. Claude is recommended, but ChatGPT or Gemini work too. Make sure you can paste text in and get responses back. That's it.
1
Sign into claude.ai (or your team's approved AI tool). Create a free account if you don't have one.
2
Create a new project or folder called "VA UX AI Training" to keep your exercises organized.
3
Bookmark this page. You'll come back each day for your next exercise.
Privacy note
Never paste PII, PHI, or sensitive VA data into any AI tool. All exercises in this program use de-identified or fictional scenarios. When applying techniques to real work, always follow your team's data handling policies.
Day 1 Learn Your First Prompting Technique ▼
This week is about getting better work out of AI than you would without it. Today's technique: set a quality bar before the AI starts work. Ask AI to define what world-class looks like, then use that to produce the work. You'll write a weekly status update to a OCTO product lead with and without this step. This exercise will take about 8 minutes.

On Day 15, we'll learn how to pull your real work into this update and automate it. For now, we're just focusing on setting a quality bar.

Why this works
Asking the AI to set success criteria before it works can improve the quality of the output. It's like conducting a design critique against an agreed-upon rubric instead of vibes.
Day 2 See the Power of Context ▼
Today you'll learn how adding VA-specific context transforms generic AI output into something actually useful. You'll compare a plain prompt to one loaded with context about your team, your users, and VA design patterns.
Day 3 Have AI Do the Research First ▼
Before asking AI to produce something, have it research the problem space. Today you'll ask AI to analyze VA.gov form patterns before drafting recommendations. The output quality difference is dramatic.
Day 4 How to Catch AI Mistakes ▼
AI confidently produces wrong answers. Today you'll learn to spot hallucinations, verify claims, and build a review habit. You'll practice by having AI describe a VADS component and fact-checking the output against design.va.gov.
Day 5 Teach It Your Standards ▼
Today you'll give AI examples of your best work and ask it to match the quality. Paste in a past research finding, design rationale, or content draft you're proud of, and have AI produce new work at the same level.
Day 6 Learn to Troubleshoot ▼
When AI gives you a bad response, most people rephrase and try again. Today you'll learn a better approach: ask the AI what went wrong and what it needs to do better. Turns out AI is great at diagnosing its own failures.
Day 7 From Chat to Action ▼
Week 1 wrap-up. Reflect on what you've learned and identify the one technique that changed how you work most. Write a 2-sentence summary of your biggest takeaway and share it in your team's Slack channel.

Week 2: Research & Synthesis

This week puts AI to work on the messy middle of UX research: processing notes, finding patterns, and turning raw data into insights.

Day 8Synthesize a Research Session▼
Take notes from a recent Veteran interview (de-identified) and ask Claude to identify the top 3 pain points, group related quotes, and suggest follow-up questions you might have missed.
Day 9Build an Affinity Map▼
Paste quotes from multiple sessions and have AI cluster them into themes. Compare the AI-generated groupings to what you would have done manually.
Day 10Draft a Research Plan▼
Use AI as a thought partner to draft a research plan. Give it your hypothesis and let it challenge your assumptions, suggest methods, and identify blind spots.
Day 11Strengthen Your Screener▼
Paste a participant screener and ask AI to identify leading questions, gaps in your criteria, and questions that might exclude relevant Veterans.
Day 12Write a Topline Summary▼
Turn raw findings into a shareable topline summary. Practice giving AI the right format, audience, and constraints to produce something you'd actually send to stakeholders.
Day 13Generate Interview Questions▼
Give AI your research objectives and have it draft a conversation guide. Critique the questions together: which are leading? Which are too broad? Which will Veterans actually answer honestly?
Day 14Research Week Retro▼
Reflect on the week. Which research task benefited most from AI? Where did you still need your own judgment? Document your top 2 reusable prompts.

Week 3: Design & Iteration

Use AI to accelerate your design work: critique flows, audit content, generate alternatives, and stress-test your decisions against VA standards.

Day 15Get a Design Critique▼
Describe a screen or flow you're working on and ask AI to critique it using VADS principles, accessibility heuristics, and Veteran mental models.
Day 16Audit for Plain Language▼
Paste a page of VA.gov content and ask AI to flag jargon, passive voice, and sentences above an 8th-grade reading level. Compare its suggestions to the VA content style guide.
Day 17Generate Microcopy Alternatives▼
Take a button label, error message, or helper text you're stuck on and ask for 5 alternatives. Give AI your constraints: character limit, tone, reading level, and context.
Day 18Map Edge Cases▼
Describe a form flow and ask AI to think of every edge case: partial data, expired sessions, screen reader paths, error states, back-button behavior. See what you missed.
Day 19Accessibility Review Checklist▼
Give AI a component description and ask it to generate a WCAG 2.2 AA checklist specific to that component. Cross-reference with the VADS accessibility guidance.
Day 20Write a Design Rationale▼
Practice using AI to draft the "why" behind a design decision. Useful for Collaboration Cycle reviews and design documentation.
Day 21Design Week Retro▼
Which design tasks benefited most from AI? Where was your own expertise irreplaceable? Update your prompt library with this week's best techniques.

Week 4: Put It All Together

Chain the techniques from the first three weeks into real workflows. Build reusable templates, integrate AI into your sprint rhythm, and design your personal toolkit.

Day 22Build a Reusable Prompt Template▼
Take your most-used prompt from the past 3 weeks and turn it into a fill-in-the-blank template your whole team can use.
Day 23Prep for a Collab Cycle Review▼
Use AI to prepare for a Collaboration Cycle touchpoint: generate anticipated questions, draft responses, and identify gaps in your artifacts.
Day 24Co-Author a Product Brief▼
Draft a product brief or initiative summary with AI as co-author. Practice the back-and-forth of refining a document together.
Day 25Create a Research Repository Entry▼
Turn raw research notes into a structured repository entry: key findings, supporting quotes, recommendations, and metadata. Build a prompt that does this consistently.
Day 26Automate Your Status Updates▼
Remember Day 1? Now combine everything: pull in real sprint data, apply the quality bar technique, add context, and produce a status update that actually impresses your product lead.
Day 27Teach a Teammate▼
Pair with someone on your team and walk them through your favorite technique. Teaching solidifies learning, and your team gets stronger together.
Day 28Design Your AI Workflow Toolkit▼
Compile your best prompts, techniques, and workflows into a personal toolkit document. This is your reference for after the program ends.
Day 29Identify What's Next▼
Ask AI to help you identify the next frontier: what tasks in your role could benefit from AI that you haven't tried yet? Make a list of experiments for the next month.
Day 30Graduation Day▼
You did it. Reflect on how your AI practice has changed over 30 days. Write a short retro (with AI, naturally) and share your biggest takeaway with your team. The training wheels are off.
