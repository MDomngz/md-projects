Start here

What we're actually doing

Our draft already names where we want to go — five themes, a set of goals, and a long list of "possible" actions. What it doesn't yet have is how: which actions we truly commit to, who carries each one, by when, and how we'll know they worked. That "how" is the work of this retreat.

Hold one idea the whole day

A goal is a direction. An action is a first step someone takes on a date. Our draft is full of directions written as "possible" and italicized — gentle, tentative, unowned. Your job today is to make a small number of them real: pick them up, put a name and a date on them, and let the rest go for now. A plan that commits to everything commits to nothing.

The building block

What a strong action looks like

Before you argue about which actions, agree on what makes one worth committing to. A strong action can answer all five of these in a sentence.

1ClearA specific thing to do, not a theme
2OwnedOne named person accountable
3A first stepSomething that can start in 30 days
4TimedA date to check on it
5MeasurableYou'll know if it worked

✕ Still a direction

"Increase Parent Ed opportunities."
No owner, no first step, no way to know if it happened.

✓ An action you can carry

"Lilia schedules two non-meeting Parent Ed evenings open to the wider community by Nov 1; success = 25+ attendees each."
Clear, owned, first step, timed, measurable.

The draft has no success measures anywhere

That's the biggest gap to fill. For every action you commit to, ask: "A year from now, what would tell us this worked?" A number, a date, or an observable change all count. If you can't name one, the action may not be ready — or may not be worth committing.

Prioritizing

Choosing the vital few

The draft lists dozens of possible actions. A board of our size can meaningfully move a handful per stream in a year. Use these three lenses to sort — out loud, together.

Impact

Does this move a goal that matters most this year — enrollment, financial stability, faculty? Prefer actions that serve more than one goal at once.

Effort & readiness

Can we actually start it now, with the people and money we have? A high-impact action we can't staff is a wish, not a plan.

Sequence

What has to come first? Some actions unlock others (a facility audit before a capital campaign). Name the order, don't run them in parallel by accident.

A simple rule for the day: for each stream, aim to leave with 3–5 committed actions — the ones that are high-impact and ready. Everything else goes to a "later / watch" list, not the trash. Naming what you're not doing this year is as valuable as naming what you are.

The terrain

The four work streams

These are your breakout groupings. For each, the point is not to fill a form — it's to think well about where the real leverage is. The prompts below are ways in, and each carries a belonging (DEIB) question through it rather than treating DEIB as separate.

1
Accredited Waldorf Education
Grow our capacity to give children a fully formed, accredited Waldorf education.

Ways to think about it

Which single investment in faculty — salary, mentoring, training, or succession — would most protect the education five years out?
The Middle School model is the knottiest item: is it a curriculum decision, a financial one, or an enrollment one? (It's all three — decide who owns the whole knot.)
What does "support for neurodivergent students" require that we don't have yet — people, training, or space?
Belonging question: Does our faculty pipeline reach beyond the people already in our circle?
2
Renewal of the Community Spirit
Energize the adults working together to educate our children.

Ways to think about it

If we could only fix one thing about how we communicate and govern, which would build the most trust?
Enrollment lives in this stream but is really the whole school's oxygen — who owns a single enrollment-and-retention plan end to end?
Where does energy already exist (Parent Guild, alumni) that we could support rather than manufacture?
Belonging question: Do our enrollment and welcome materials speak to families who don't yet see themselves here?
3
Community Outreach
Partner beyond SWS to share our gifts, to the benefit of all.

Ways to think about it

Of the many partners named (Horn Farm, Lancaster Conservancy, Marietta Borough, Marietta Live, the Food Bank), which two or three deepen the curriculum and raise our visibility? We can't resource all of them.
Is outreach here a marketing effort, a service effort, or a relationship effort — and who holds the calendar and budget?
What's the smallest visible presence in Marietta that would make us feel like part of the town?
Belonging question: The Susquehannock land statement sits in DEIB but the relationship lives here — should this stream carry it?
4
Resources
Strengthen our human and financial footing to support everything above.

Ways to think about it

We use three names — emergency fund, Contingency Fund, reserve. Are these one pool or three? Settle the language before deciding dollars.
A capital campaign is named several ways. What has to be true (facility plan, development capacity, board readiness) before we could launch one?
Which single administrative or facility role would relieve the most pressure if we filled or defined it?
Belonging question: Do our scholarship and financial-aid structures actually widen who can afford to be here?
The thread

DEIB is a lens, not a stream

Rather than a separate group, belonging runs through all four streams. As you shape actions, carry these three questions like a thread through the day.

Who's missing?

In this action, whose voice or presence isn't yet at the table — as a family, a hire, a partner, a board member?

Who does it serve?

Does this widen access and welcome, or quietly keep things comfortable for those already here?

Where does it show?

Is our commitment visible in the curriculum, the budget, and the language — or only in our statement?

Where streams touch

Mind the seams

The streams aren't independent. The most important decisions live between groups — resolve these together, not inside a single breakout.

Middle School (1) ↔ Enrollment (2): the model's math depends on enrollment growth. One decision, made together.
Enrollment & scholarships (2) ↔ Budget & EITC (4): financial aid and "cost to educate" span community and resources.
Outreach marketing (3) ↔ Internal communications (2): one message, one calendar, one owner — not two.
Staffing, steward & succession (1 & 4) ↔ HR (4): role definitions and evaluation cut across education and resources.
Watch for

Traps that turn plans into paper

✕
Re-writing the goals. The goals are set. Spend your energy on the actions underneath them.
✕
Committing to everything. Enthusiasm for all 30 actions means none gets carried. Choose.
✕
Actions with no name. "The board will…" is nobody. Every commitment needs one owner.
✕
No first step. If nothing happens in the next 30 days, it isn't really committed yet.
✕
No way to know. Without a measure, next year we'll debate whether it happened instead of acting.
✕
Leaving seams inside one group. Cross-stream calls (enrollment, money, comms) must surface in plenary.
So we speak the same way

Shared words for the day

Commit / Defer / CutThe three fates of any proposed action. Commit = we do it this year with an owner. Defer = later or watch. Cut = not for us right now.
OwnerThe one person accountable for an action moving — not the one who does all the work, the one who won't let it stall.
First stepThe concrete thing that happens within ~30 days. Proof an action is real.
Success measureWhat we'd point to a year from now to say it worked — a number, a date, or a visible change.
The vital fewThe 3–5 actions per stream worth our real attention this year.
SeamA decision that spans two streams and must be resolved together.
Cost to EducateThe true per-student cost — a shared vocabulary for why tuition is underwritten by fundraising.
