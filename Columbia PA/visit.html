Three centuries of craft and commerce didn't end with the ferry. They moved indoors — into a town of restored historic buildings now filled with dealers, makers, and treasure.

There is no better year to come than the town's 300th. Columbia rewards an unhurried day: start with the stone house where Susanna Wright kept her silk and her letters, walk down to the river where the great bridge once stood, and lose an afternoon among the antique markets that earned the town its modern name.

Signature Room

Susanna's Desk

Wright's Ferry Mansion — the 1738 stone house at Second and Cherry — is the anchor of any visit. It was Susanna Wright's home and the base of a remarkable life: silkworms and mulberry, proto-feminist verse, and a correspondence with Benjamin Franklin that traded books for casks of pickled Susquehanna salmon.

On this site, the room called Susanna's Desk stages that life like an open archive drawer — her silk to Queen Charlotte, her poetry, her letters. In person, the mansion's furnishings and stonework do the same. Read her full story →

The Modern Trade

The antique markets

Columbia is recognized as an antique capital, with more than a dozen large shops set in restored historic buildings — hidden gems and genuine treasure for an afternoon of wandering. A few of the destinations:

Burning Bridge Antiques Market

A sprawling market named for the town's most famous night — multiple floors of dealers.

Tollbooth Antiques

A favorite stop filled with finds, in a building with its own history.

Partners & Friends Antique Center

Two floors and more than 35 dealers, with friendly staff to point the way.

Old State Theatre Antique Mall

An antique mall and art gallery in a former theatre — atmosphere included.

Bootleg Antiques

One more of the town's well-loved shops for treasure-hunting.

…and more

Over a dozen markets in all, clustered in walkable, restored historic buildings.

Shop names, dealers, and hours change. Check current listings before planning your route.

Where It All Happened

The riverfront & the piers

Walk down to the Susquehanna and you can still see the stone piers that once carried the longest covered bridge in the world — the bones of the span the town burned in 1863. The riverfront and rail-trail make for a quiet, affecting end to a visit: the same water that carried the ferry, the freedom seekers, and the firelight of that June night. The story of the bridge →

Editorial standard

This is a draft built for a creative brief. Every factual claim should be re-verified against a primary or institutional source before publication. Where folklore is involved — such as Susanna Wright's silk reaching Queen Charlotte — the text marks it as tradition rather than settled fact.

Guardrail — two Columbias

This site concerns Columbia, Pennsylvania. The 1834 riots described here are not the 20th-century events in Columbia, Tennessee. The 1946 "Mink Slide" riot belongs to Maury County, Tennessee, and is documented separately; we keep the two histories distinct.
